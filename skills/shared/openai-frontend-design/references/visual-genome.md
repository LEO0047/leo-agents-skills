# Visual Genome

A thinking frame for the direction pass, not a preset. It separates five things a look is made of, so you choose each one for this page instead of inheriting a previous project's whole package. Every axis may be `none`; a chosen `none` is a decision, and no axis is filled just to have something there.

## The five axes

### 1. Physical world

One world that could be built, photographed, or consistently illustrated. It sets material, gravity, wear, scale, and where the light comes from. A product studio, a repair shop, a material library, a bright archive, a humid night workshop, a paper-cut stage: reasoning prompts, not a menu. When a section needs contrast, change camera distance, light, or density before changing worlds.

### 2. Graphic language

One primary system for type, grid, rules, labels, and density, with at most one contrasting secondary language at a real narrative boundary. Catalogue, maintenance manual, scientific publication, fashion editorial, children's book, street fabrication marks. Structural devices (numbers, coordinates, warnings, diagrams) encode something true about the content.

### 3. Display medium

Zero or one local viewing treatment: monochrome or color CRT, industrial LCD, e-paper, thermal paper, projection, oscilloscope. It appears on selected surfaces or states as a camera or signal layer. The site's aesthetic comes from the other axes.

### 4. Motion grammar

One motion logic drawn from the subject (assembly, signal startup, camera tracking, print registration, scanning, tape search, toolpath, unfolding paper) or `none`. A stable page with one story turn beats a page where everything floats independently.

### 5. Image transformation

One primary treatment and at most one supporting one: photography, painted illustration, dithering, halftone, wireframe, thermal, X-ray or section, photocopy contrast, registration shift, depth map, lens refraction. The treatment reveals material, state, or narrative time; used as a surface filter it reads as a template.

## Anti-copy comparison

Before committing, compare the proposed direction with up to three recent completed directions you can actually see (in the workspace, this conversation, or explicitly provided notes) across: dominant palette, display-type character, layout skeleton, hero composition, motion grammar, image treatment, signature interaction.

When three or more repeat as a bundle, change at least one axis and say what changed. Repeating one justified habit is fine; repeating a whole package is the template this frame exists to prevent. With no history available, write `Uncertainty: recent visual-direction evidence unavailable` and instead check the direction against the base frontend skill's three AI-default looks.

Brand research informs transferable principles (content order, contrast, camera behavior, sequencing, material response). Layouts, identities, slogans, product language, and complete trade dress stay with their owners, and brand names stay out of prompts unless the user asks for a lawful comparison.
